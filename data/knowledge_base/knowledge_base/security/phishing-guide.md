# Phishing Email Identification Guide

Phishing emails are fraudulent messages designed to steal credentials, install malware, or trick you into revealing sensitive information.

## How to Identify a Phishing Email

### Suspicious Sender Address
- Hover over the sender's display name to reveal the actual email address.
- Legitimate company emails end in `@company.com`. Watch for lookalikes like `@company-support.com` or `@company.co`.
- External sender banners (shown by Outlook) on internal-looking emails are a red flag.

### Urgent or Threatening Language
- "Your account will be suspended in 24 hours"
- "Immediate action required"
- "Verify your identity NOW"
Legitimate IT communications never require immediate action via email alone.

### Suspicious Links
- Hover over links before clicking. The URL shown in the status bar must match the displayed text.
- Watch for URL shorteners (bit.ly, tinyurl) in official communications.
- Legitimate IT portals use `*.company.com` domains only.

### Unexpected Attachments
- Never open `.exe`, `.zip`, `.js`, `.vbs` attachments from unknown senders.
- Even `.pdf` and `.docx` files can contain macros.
- If unexpected, verify with the sender via phone or Slack before opening.

### Grammar and Spelling Errors
- Professional communications from IT and vendors are proofread. Multiple errors indicate phishing.

## What to Do If You Suspect a Phishing Email

1. **Do NOT click any links or open attachments.**
2. **Do NOT reply** to the email or call any phone numbers in it.
3. **Report it immediately:**
   - In Outlook: Use the "Report Phishing" button in the ribbon.
   - Alternatively, forward to `security@company.com` with subject "PHISHING REPORT".
4. **Delete the email** from your inbox and trash folder.
5. If you already clicked a link or entered credentials:
   - **Change your password immediately** at `https://password.company.com`.
   - **Call the IT Security Hotline: ext. 5911** (available 24/7).
   - Do NOT wait — rapid response limits damage.

## Common Phishing Scenarios in Our Environment

- Fake Microsoft 365 login pages requesting your company password.
- Fake IT helpdesk emails asking you to install a "security update" tool.
- Invoice fraud emails pretending to be from Finance or a known vendor.
- Package delivery notifications with malicious tracking links.

If in doubt, report it. False positives are always better than a data breach.
